# Research Limitations

## Magnetic loss and material assumptions

The final coefficients \(k_h\), \(k_e\) and \(n\) are illustrative. The reference powers were selected at 50 Hz, 1.20 T peak and 0.35 mm thickness, then converted to coefficients. They were not calibrated to a measured loop, sheet or transformer. The low, medium and high cases are sensitivity cases and do not express confidence intervals. They select different minimum-loss candidates. A mathematical result below 1.00 T remains covered by the model but may miss minor-loop effects. High induction can invalidate uniform flux and the linearised circuit approximation. The classical eddy current term assumes sufficiently thin electrically isolated sheets and a sinusoidal flux waveform. Excess dynamic core losses are excluded.

## Geometry, mass and manufacture

The homogeneous-flux E–I model uses fixed-proportion scaling, uniform stack factor and rectangular winding turns. It omits joint and corner flux crowding, nonuniform lamination paths and manufacturing effects. The mean turn length is layer-weighted, without actual winding drawings. Core mass includes assumed steel only. Bare copper mass includes the modelled leads and excludes insulation and assembly hardware. Neither estimate was verified by weighing a physical build. The window, current density and radial build data are diagnostic. Manufacturing fit, creepage, clearance, insulation class, tolerances and applicable compliance remain unverified.

## Electrical load, heating and omitted losses

The circuit uses a sinusoidal stiff 230 V RMS source and a self-consistent 100 VA, unity-power-factor load. Excitation current, core-loss current and leakage reactance are omitted from its loaded-voltage calculation. Core losses are added separately to the modelled loss objective. The resulting winding currents and voltage predictions describe the stated circuit. Copper resistivity uses a fixed assumed 60 °C winding temperature without self-heating, hot spots or thermal feedback. Skin and proximity effects, stray losses, dielectric dissipation, excess magnetic loss and other loss mechanisms are not included. The study contains no experimental transformer measurements.

## Screening and numerical scope

Eligibility depends only on nominal no-drop peak ≤1.50 T and loaded voltage ≥22.0 V RMS. The terminal voltage has no upper screen, and target-voltage error does not enter the objective. The flux screen does not quantify supply tolerance, inrush or local saturation. The discrete domain spans 450–1100 primary turns and 10–30 cm², at 25-turn and 0.5 cm² increments. An interior grid minimum establishes a conditional result at the imposed resolution. The low-scenario lower-boundary point leaves smaller turn counts unexamined. These findings support a portfolio discussion of modelled trade-offs, with no claim of real-world efficiency or manufacturing readiness.
