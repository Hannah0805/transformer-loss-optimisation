## Simplifications and consequences

- **Supply and load.** Sinusoidal steady-state operation at nominal unity power factor underlies the first model. Harmonics, DC bias, inrush, energisation transients, and thermal or fault protection are outside scope. The approximation \(E_p\approx V_p\) can degrade with large voltage drops or excitation current. The model describes a nominal load point and requires an explicit regulation check.
- **Magnetic circuit.** The model uses representative, spatially uniform peak induction in the steel and ideal flux split between return limbs. It omits local flux concentration, nonuniform flux through joints and across the stack, air-gap fringing and anisotropy. A magnetisation curve and a steady-state margin can screen candidates. They cannot establish local saturation or prototype performance.
- **Copper.** Use DC winding resistance with each winding represented by a mean turn length and uniform temperature. AC resistance increases from skin and proximity effects, leakage-flux-related winding eddies and other stray load loss are excluded. A specified-temperature sensitivity can vary winding resistance without solving self-heating, winding hot spots or temperature feedback. Temperature dependence of magnetic properties is excluded from the initial model.
- **Core.** Classical eddy current loss assumes electrically isolated sheets and weak magnetic skin effect. Hysteresis uses a quasi-static loop or an explicitly declared approximation over a stated range. The separated objective omits excess dynamic core loss, interlaminar currents and joint-associated losses. The aggregate comparison, if used, inherits the total-loss content of the sheet data.
- **Other losses and regulation.** Stray losses in structural parts and dielectric losses are excluded. Leakage reactance and other leakage-flux effects are excluded from the first regulation calculation. The three-loss model cannot predict all sources of measured input power or full-load efficiency.
- **Manufacturing uncertainties.** Lamination cutting, burrs, coating defects, stacking, clamping stress, steel-lot variation, conductor tolerances and assembly dimensions can affect actual loss and feasibility. A sheet guarantee applies only at its stated test condition. Finished-core and winding data would be required to validate assembly-level predictions.
- **Interpretation.** Values from the brief are specifications or starting values. Manufacturer values are labelled typical or guaranteed. Geometry choices and illustrative scenarios are assumptions. Unit conversions and analytical checks are derived quantities. Future computed losses are model predictions. A model minimum is conditional on the inputs, excluded mechanisms, constraints and searched range. The model cannot establish a global real-transformer efficiency optimum.
